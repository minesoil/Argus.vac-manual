00 — Read First
OnShape Basics
Before building anything, here are the key concepts you'll use throughout this guide. Read this once — it explains every tool referenced in every part below.
Term / ToolWhat it meansHow to access it
Part StudioA workspace where you model one or more 3D parts. Think of it as a blank workshop.Click the + tab at the bottom of the screen → "Part Studio"
SketchA flat 2D drawing (lines, circles, rectangles) that you later turn into a 3D shape.Top toolbar → Sketch icon, then click a flat surface or a plane
PlaneAn invisible flat surface to sketch on. Every document has Top, Front, and Right planes.Visible in the left feature tree under "Origin"
Dimension (D)The tool that lets you type in exact sizes for your sketch lines and circles.Sketch toolbar → Dimension, or press D
ExtrudePushes a flat sketch shape into a 3D solid — like pressing dough into a mould.Top toolbar → Extrude after finishing a sketch
RevolveSpins a sketch around an axis to create a round shape — like a lathe.Top toolbar → Revolve
Boolean Remove (Cut)Subtracts one shape from another — used to make holes and slots.In Extrude, change "New" to Remove
FilletRounds off a sharp edge.Top toolbar → Fillet
Circular PatternCopies a feature or solid around a central axis (e.g. 8 screws around a circle).Top toolbar → Circular Pattern
Linear PatternCopies a feature in a straight line (e.g. 23 filter pleats in a row).Top toolbar → Linear Pattern
MirrorFlips a part to create a perfect mirror image.Top toolbar → Mirror
AssemblyA workspace where you bring all your parts together and position them.Click the + tab → "Assembly"
MateA constraint in the Assembly that locks two parts together or lets them move in a defined way.Assembly toolbar → Mate
Fastened MateLocks two parts rigidly together with no movement.Mate → choose type "Fastened"
💡
Navigation shortcuts: Scroll wheel = zoom in/out. Middle-click drag = pan. Right-click drag = rotate the view. Press F to fit everything on screen.
📐
Units: Every measurement in this guide is in millimetres (mm). When you create a new document, go to the bottom-right corner of a Part Studio and confirm the units are set to millimeter.
⚠️
Always finish your sketch before extruding. Click the green checkmark (✓) in the toolbar to exit sketch mode. If your sketch lines are blue they are unconstrained — add dimensions until they turn black.
01 — Setup
Create Your Document
One document will hold all your parts and the final assembly. Do this once before building anything.
Go to onshape.com and sign in (free account is fine)
Click Create → Document. Name it AIVac and click OK.
Set units to millimetres
In your new Part Studio, look at the bottom-right of the screen. Click the units indicator and choose millimeter. Do this in every new Part Studio you create.
Create one Part Studio tab for each part listed below
Click the + button at the bottom of the screen → Part Studio. Name each tab to match the part name. You'll need: Chassis Base, Outer Shell, Bumper, Drive Wheel, NEMA17 Stepper, Caster Wheel, Raspberry Pi 4, Arduino Mega, A4988 Driver, Battery Pack, Buck Converter, Pi Camera, LiDAR A1, Vacuum Module, Wiring Harness, Assembly.
✅
Recommended build order: Work through the parts in the order in this guide. Structural parts first, then electronics, then put everything together in the Assembly at the end.
Part 01
Chassis Base Deck
chassisBase.kcl
The main circular platform that everything mounts on. It's a round disc with standoff posts and a front opening for the vacuum intake.
Step A — Main Disc
Open the "Chassis Base" Part Studio tab. Click Sketch → click the Top plane.
You'll see the grid appear. You're now drawing on the flat top view.
Draw a circle centred at the origin (0, 0)
In the sketch toolbar click Circle. Click once at the centre cross (origin point) to set the centre, then click somewhere to the right to set the radius. Press D, click the circle edge, and type 236 for the diameter (OnShape uses diameter for circles by default). Press Enter. The circle turns black.
Draw the front intake slot inside the circle
Still in the same sketch: click Corner Rectangle. Draw a rectangle anywhere, then use Dimension (D) to set:
Width: 126 mm
Height: 22 mm
Centre it horizontally: add a Midpoint constraint between the rectangle's top edge midpoint and the vertical axis (Y-axis line)
Position it: dimension from the X-axis to the bottom edge of the rectangle = −131 mm (so the rectangle sits at y = −120 to −142). More precisely: dimension from the origin to the bottom of the rectangle = 131 mm downward.
Click the green checkmark ✓ to exit the sketch
Extrude the circle (disc only) upward 8 mm
Click Extrude. Click inside the big circle but outside the rectangle to select only the disc region. Set depth to 8 mm, direction Blind, click the upward arrow. Click the green checkmark.
Cut the intake slot through the disc
Click Sketch → click the top flat face of the disc. Draw a rectangle 126 × 22 mm centred at x=0, y=−120 mm (measure from origin). ✓ Exit sketch → click Extrude → select the rectangle region → change type to Remove → set depth to 8 mm → click the downward direction. ✓
Step B — 8 Standoff Posts
Sketch the 4 Pi standoff circles on the top face
Click Sketch → click the top face of the disc. Draw 4 small circles with diameter 8.4 mm (radius 4.2 mm) at these XY positions:
Pi Standoff 1: x = −76, y = 14
Pi Standoff 2: x = −18, y = 14 (−76 + 58 = −18)
Pi Standoff 3: x = −76, y = 63 (14 + 49 = 63)
Pi Standoff 4: x = −18, y = 63
Use the Point tool or just place each circle centre and dimension it from the origin. ✓ Exit sketch.
Sketch the 4 Arduino standoff circles
On the same top face, draw 4 more circles diameter 8.4 mm at:
Arduino Standoff 1: x = 31, y = 18
Arduino Standoff 2: x = 103, y = 18 (31 + 72 = 103)
Arduino Standoff 3: x = 31, y = 60 (18 + 42 = 60)
Arduino Standoff 4: x = 103, y = 60
✓ Exit sketch.
Extrude all 8 standoff circles upward 10 mm
Extrude → click all 8 circle regions → depth 10 mm → type Add → upward direction. ✓
Step C — Threaded Insert Holes (Pi side only)
Sketch 4 small circles on top of the 4 Pi standoffs
Sketch → click the top face of a Pi standoff. Draw a circle diameter 5.2 mm centred at the standoff's centre. Repeat for all 4 Pi standoffs. ✓ Exit sketch.
Cut the insert holes 5 mm deep
Extrude → select all 4 small circles → type Remove → depth 5 mm downward. ✓
Step D — Intake Seal Ledges
Add 3 small rubber-seal strips around the intake opening
Sketch on top face → draw 3 rectangles and extrude them upward 4 mm:
Back strip: 126 × 5 mm centred at x=0, y=−105
Left strip: 5 × 34 mm centred at x=−63, y=−122
Right strip: 5 × 34 mm centred at x=+63, y=−122
✓ Extrude all three 4 mm upward.
💡
Finished chassis disc: Diameter 236 mm, thickness 8 mm, with a 126 × 22 mm front slot, 8 standoff posts, and 4 threaded-insert holes in the Pi-side posts.

Part 03
Front Bumper
bumper.kcl
Three rubber pads wrapping the front of the robot, with three dark sensor windows. The two side pads are angled at ±34°.
Sketch the front pad on the Top plane
Rectangle 124 × 13 mm centred at x=0, y=−151. ✓ Extrude 18 mm upward.
Sketch the left angled pad
Sketch on Top plane → rectangle 76 × 13 mm. Place it with its centre at x=−96, y=−128. Use the Transform tool (or after extruding, use Move Face) to rotate it −34° around the Z-axis. ✓ Extrude 18 mm.

Part 04
Drive Wheel — build once, mirror for second wheel
driveWheel.kcl
A 65 mm rubber tyre with a plastic hub, shaft adapter, and 18 raised tread blocks around the circumference. The wheel spins around the X-axis (left–right axis).
Tyre
Sketch a circle on the Front plane (the XZ plane)
Circle centred at origin, diameter 65 mm. ✓ Extrude (symmetric both ways) 24 mm total — set Symmetric in the Extrude dialog so it goes 12 mm each way along the X-axis.
Plastic Hub
Sketch a smaller circle on the Front plane, diameter 36 mm
Centred at origin. ✓ Extrude symmetric 28 mm total (14 mm each side). This sits inside the tyre.
Shaft Adapter
Sketch a circle diameter 10 mm on the Front plane, centred at origin
✓ Extrude symmetric 30 mm total. This is the metal insert that slides onto the motor shaft.
Tread Blocks
Sketch one tread block on the Front plane
Draw a rectangle 5 × 4 mm positioned so its inner edge sits at radius 32.5 mm from origin (place it at y = 32.5 mm, centred). ✓ Extrude it symmetric 26 mm total (width of tyre + 2 mm overhang).
Circular Pattern the tread block: 18 copies, full 360° around X-axis
Select the tread extrude → Circular Pattern → axis = X-axis → instances = 18 → 360°. ✓ You now have evenly-spaced tread all around the tyre.
Create the Right Wheel
In the Assembly (or by duplicating the tab), simply use the same part for both wheels
The drive wheel is symmetrical — you don't need a separate part. In the Assembly you'll place one copy on the left at x=−151 mm and one on the right at x=+151 mm. No mirroring needed.
Part 05
NEMA 17 Stepper Motor
nema17Stepper.kcl
The 42.3 mm square motor body. Build the body, shaft, pilot boss, and 4 mounting screw heads on the front face. Use the same part for both left and right — mirror in the Assembly.
Motor body: sketch a square on the Front plane
Rectangle 42.3 × 42.3 mm centred at origin. ✓ Extrude symmetric 34 mm total (17 mm each way along X). This is the main steel body.
Front and rear end plates (thin silver caps)
Sketch the same 42.3 × 42.3 mm square. Extrude it 2.4 mm from the left face of the body (outward), then repeat from the right face. These are the two thin plates.
Pilot boss: circular hub on the left (front) face
Sketch on the left flat face → circle diameter 22 mm centred at the face centre. ✓ Extrude 4 mm outward (away from body).
Output shaft: thinner cylinder extending further left
Sketch on the end face of the pilot boss → circle diameter 5 mm centred. ✓ Extrude 20 mm outward. This is the D-shaft.
Four M3 screw heads on the front plate face
Sketch on the front face → draw 4 small circles diameter 4.8 mm at positions ±15.5 mm in both Y and Z (the NEMA 17 mount pitch is 31 mm, so the holes are at ±15.5 from centre):
Top-left: y=+15.5, z=+15.5
Top-right: y=−15.5, z=+15.5
Bottom-left: y=+15.5, z=−15.5
Bottom-right: y=−15.5, z=−15.5
✓ Extrude 1.6 mm outward (these are raised screw heads).
Lead wires (optional visual detail)
Sketch on the right (rear) face → draw 4 thin rectangles 1.8 × 1.8 mm spaced 7 mm apart vertically. ✓ Extrude 44 mm outward. These represent the 4 motor lead wires.
🔁
Build this once. In the Assembly, place one copy at x=−101 mm (left motor) pointing left, and a second copy rotated 180° around Z placed at x=+101 mm (right motor) pointing right.

17 — Final Step
Full Assembly
Now bring all the parts together. The global origin (0,0,0) is at floor level under the centre of the robot. All Z positions below are measured from that floor point.

Assembly coordinate system

Origin is at the robot's floor-level centre. +Y = rear of robot (battery/dustbin side). −Y = front (bumper/camera side). +Z = up. +X = right side of robot when viewed from above.

Open the Assembly tab. Click "Insert" → insert every part you've built.
Parts appear in the top-left panel. Drag them onto the canvas. You'll position them precisely using the Fasten Mate.
Fix the Chassis Base as the "ground" part
Right-click the chassis → Fix. This locks it in place as the anchor for everything else. Its reference point goes at x=0, y=0, z=+18 mm.

How to Fasten Each Part (step-by-step for one example — repeat for all)
Example: Fasten the Outer Shell at z=+49 mm
Click Mate in the Assembly toolbar.
Choose type: Fastened.
Click the bottom face of the Outer Shell — this is the Mate connector on the shell side.
Click the top face of the Chassis Base at its centre — this is the Mate connector on the chassis side.
In the offset fields that appear, type Z offset = 31 mm (shell bottom should sit 31 mm above the chassis top face, since chassis top is at z=26 mm and shell sits at z=49+offset).
Click ✓. The shell snaps into place.
Tip: For precise placement, use the Offset values in the Mate dialog instead of trying to drag parts by hand.
For parts that need pre-rotation (Battery Pack, Right Motor, Camera)
First insert the part. Before mating, click the part in the canvas → Edit in place or right-click → Move/Rotate. Apply the rotation (90° or 180° as noted in the table) around the correct axis. Then apply the Fastened Mate to lock its final position.
Check your assembly by hiding the outer shell
In the parts list on the left, click the eye icon next to the Outer Shell to hide it. You should see all the electronics, motors, wheels, and vacuum module laid out inside the chassis. Check nothing is floating or intersecting badly.
Done — save and name your assembly "AIVac_Main_Assembly"
Press Ctrl+S (or Cmd+S on Mac). Your complete AIVac robot vacuum is fully assembled in OnShape with real-scale dimensions from all 20 KCL source files.
⚠️
Collision check: After assembly, use Interference Detection (Tools menu → Interference Detection) to find any parts that are overlapping. Common issues: drive wheel hub vs chassis edge, and the dustbin vs the Arduino standoffs. Adjust part positions slightly in the mate offsets to resolve.
🎉
Finished! Robot total footprint: Ø 296 mm. Total height (chassis floor to LiDAR top): approx 148 mm. Wheel track width (wheel CL to CL): 302 mm.
