## ppt/slides/slide2.xml
The Problem
Traditional robot vacuums rely solely on LIDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) or bump sensors. They can map walls, but they blindly run over small, complex obstacles like charging cables, socks, or poop, causing damage to the robot or the home.

## ppt/slides/slide3.xml
Solution
Integrate a lightweight Machine Learning model (TensorFlow Lite) via a front-facing camera.
Before the vacuum approaches an area, the camera scans the floor. If it identifies a "high-risk" small object (like a phone charger), it dynamically overrides the ROS (Robot Operating System) navigation stack to establish a "no-go" zone around that specific object.

## ppt/slides/slide4.xml
How does a vacuum work?
A high speed motor spins an impeller fan(葉輪風
扇) at high speeds around 30,000 RPM. This spinning fan creates a low pressure zone inside the dustbin area. As nature naturally wants to equalize pressure, air from the higher pressure areas( the areas where you want to suck up the debris) rushes in to equalize the low pressure area, carrying dust and debris with it.

## ppt/slides/slide9.xml
Development Roadmap
Phase 0(Design): Order parts, design cad, some more further planning
Phase 1 ( Hardware & Kinematics):  Cut chassis, mount motors, 
establish basic remote control movement using Arduino
Phase 2 (ROS & Mapping): Connect Raspberry Pi, configure the ROS navigation stack, and get the LiDAR to generate a 2D map of a room
Phase 3 (AI): Train a basic object detection model using images of cables and socks and things you wouldn’t want the robot to suck up by accident. Mount the camera and test the accuracy of the detection system
Phase 4(Integration & Vacuum): Merge the AI data with the ROS navigation map. Install vacuum and dustbin
